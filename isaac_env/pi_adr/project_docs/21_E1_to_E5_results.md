## E1 — Long-run exploration collapse: **primary criterion MET**

**Question.** Is the vanilla (human) reward's precision failure in Stage 1 a training-length-dependent exploration-collapse artifact that an entropy bonus prevents?
**Design.** Vanilla reward, no DR, 2048 envs, **5000 iterations**, `entropy_coef` 0.005 (default) vs 0.02, seeds 42, 123, 7, 1, 2; checkpoints evaluated at iterations 200, 400, 800, 1500, 3000, 5000 (nominal physics, frozen metrics) and, exploratorily, DRshift at 800 and 5000.

### Primary criterion (pre-registered): at iteration 5000, entropy 0.02 has lower mean error AND lower worst-decile error, each exact permutation p < 0.05 (5 vs 5)

| at 5000 iterations, nominal | entropy 0.005 (per seed 42, 123, 7, 1, 2) | entropy 0.02 (per seed) | diff | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean error (mm) | **61.8 ± 22.4**: 60.1, 52.5, 49.4, 46.3, 100.7 | **30.4 ± 3.1**: 27.8, 28.6, 34.4, 28.3, 33.1 | −31.4 | **0.0079** |
| worst-decile error (mm) | **345.6 ± 233.1**: 344, 261, 223, 156, 744 | **65.6 ± 21.2**: 53, 49, 92, 49, 85 | −280.0 | **0.0079** |

**PRIMARY CRITERION MET.** p = 0.0079 is the smallest attainable value at 5 vs 5: every entropy-0.02 seed is better than every entropy-0.005 seed on both metrics. (The two bad 0.005 seeds, 100.7 mm / 744 mm for seed 2, are the largest contributors; the separation holds without them — see the sensitivity analysis.)

**Sensitivity (pre-registered): excluding the two resumed runs** (0.005 seeds 42 and 1): 0.005 seeds {123, 7, 2} vs 0.02 all five seeds: mean error p = 0.018 and worst-decile p = 0.018 (the smallest value possible for 3 vs 5, i.e. still complete separation); against the same three 0.02 seeds p = 0.10 (the smallest possible for 3 vs 3). Conclusion unchanged.

### Secondary criteria
- **S1 (collapse with training; entropy 0.005, 2 cm success 5000 vs 800):** declines in **5 of 5 seeds** (−6.2, −16.8, −16.0, −14.6, −15.3 points; mean 28.99 → 15.20 %); exact one-sided sign test p = 0.031 — **met**. Mean error rises in all five (+19, +18, +15, +3, +64 mm).
- **S2 (mechanism — action entropy at the end of training, mean of last 20 epochs of `losses/entropy`):** entropy 0.005: −3.62, −3.47, −4.02, −3.54, −4.47; entropy 0.02: −0.56, +2.00, +0.10, +2.16, +1.68. Every 0.02 run ends with higher action entropy than every 0.005 run, i.e. the default policy's exploration collapsed (action spread σ roughly e^−3.5 ≈ 0.03) while the 0.02 policy retained it — **met**.
- **S3 (dose-response; mean error in mm, mean ± sd, n = 5 except where noted):**

| iteration | entropy 0.005 | entropy 0.02 |
|---|---|---|
| 200 | 43.4 ± 14.6 | 42.2 ± 14.6 |
| 400 | 42.7 ± 10.1 | 36.4 ± 7.8 |
| 800 | 37.8 ± 4.0 | 33.7 ± 4.8 |
| 1500 | 41.3 ± 9.0 | 44.5 ± 33.9 (one transient spike, seed 123: 105 mm) |
| 3000 | 41.5 ± 10.1 | 35.3 ± 11.0 |
| 5000 | **61.8 ± 22.4** | **30.4 ± 3.1** |

  (From `results/E1_analysis.md`, all 60 nominal evaluations present and valid.) The two arms are statistically indistinguishable at 800 iterations (p ≥ 0.18, matching the earlier n = 5 test) and separate only as training lengthens: the default degrades, the entropy-0.02 arm holds or improves. The 2 cm success curve tells the same story (5000: 15.2 ± 5.3 % vs 25.1 ± 7.3 %).
- **S4 (exploratory, DRshift — hard unseen physics):** at 5000 iterations entropy 0.02 is much better: mean error 61.9 ± 14.2 vs 127.1 ± 33.9 mm (p = 0.0079) and worst-decile 345.9 ± 152.3 vs 903.0 ± 221.2 mm (p = 0.0079); at 800 iterations 52.3 vs 87.1 mm (p = 0.0079) and 384 vs 666 mm (p = 0.024).

### What E1 means
- **An explanatory success under the pre-registered rule** (interpretation table: primary met and S1 met): *with the default entropy coefficient, long PPO training on the human reward degrades precision and tail behaviour as exploration collapses; a four-times-larger entropy bonus prevents the degradation.* The effect is large (tail error about five times smaller), replicated in 5 of 5 seeds, has a measured mechanism (S2), a dose-response (S3), and carries over to the hard unseen physics (S4).
- It **explains why Stage 1's long vanilla run looked bad** (13.28 % at 2 cm after 5000 iterations at the default entropy) and why the earlier 800-iteration test found no entropy effect: the collapse has not yet happened at 800 iterations.
- **What it does not show.** (i) It concerns the human reward on the 2-DOF task; it does not test the Eureka reward, RAPP, adaptive DR or the PINN. (ii) Stage 1's *reported* human-reward numbers (98 % at 2 cm, 80 % at 1 cm) are still not reproduced: even the best E1 arm reaches ≈ 25 % at 2 cm. (iii) It is one task, one architecture, one optimiser setting; generality is untested. (iv) Whether a still-larger or scheduled entropy bonus would help is untested.

## E2 — LEAP robustness curve (existing checkpoints, 36 evaluations)
Rotations per 20 s, mean over 3 seeds, at frozen ADR 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25: L0 8.42, 8.21, 5.60, 5.50, 1.45, 0.56; L1 8.68, 8.54, 5.46, 5.37, 0.99, 0.75; L2 8.00, 7.87, 6.31, 6.16, 3.49, 1.53. **Pre-registered primary (normalised area under the curve): L0 5.05 ± 1.51, L1 5.02 ± 1.58, L2 5.72 ± 1.79; all permutation p ≥ 0.80; the hypothesised ordering L2 ≥ L1 ≥ L0 not consistent (L1 ≈ L0)** — criterion not met. Curve shape is common to all conditions: flat to ADR 5, a ≈ 35 % drop by ADR 10, flat to 15, a cliff at 20, floor at 25.

## E4 step-0 — quality-before-quantity diagnostic (54 evaluations; consistency check against earlier rotation values PASSED after replacing 2 invalid runs)
**Pre-registered criterion** (a condition has a gap if at some ADR where rotations are still ≥ 80 % of the ADR-0 value the falls per 20 s are ≥ 2× the ADR-0 value and ≥ 0.5 higher, in ≥ 2 of 3 seeds; E4 is supported if ≥ 2 of 3 conditions show a gap): **0 of 3 conditions show a gap → the diagnostic does NOT support building E4.** Falls rise (L0 0.28, L1 0.31 per 20 s at ADR 10) at the same difficulty where rotations fall, i.e. the LEAP ADR quantity gate is aligned with the quality collapse at this budget; the 2-DOF coarse-gate failure mode does not transfer. **E4 (precision-aware ADR criterion, ≈ 40 GPU hours) was therefore not built**, as pre-declared.

**Exploratory observation (NOT pre-registered; seen only after the diagnostic):** the warm-started condition L2 drops the cube far less often than L0 and L1 at every difficulty from 10 up. Falls per 20 s (mean, per-seed in brackets): ADR 10 — L0 0.28, L1 0.31, **L2 0.02** [0.02, 0.02, 0.00]; ADR 15 — 0.26, 0.31, **0.03**; ADR 20 — 1.27, 1.78, **0.23** [0.47, 0.16, 0.06]; ADR 25 — 3.23, 6.87, **1.07** [2.13, 0.76, 0.30]. At all four levels every L2 seed is below every L0 seed and every L1 seed (complete separation; the smallest attainable p with 3 vs 3 is 0.10, so none is significant). L2 also has the highest rotations at ADR 20 (3.49 vs 1.45 / 0.99). The four levels are correlated measurements of one underlying behaviour, not four independent confirmations. **This is a hypothesis, not a finding**: warm-starting ADR at a harder difficulty may make LEAP policies more reliable (fewer drops) under hard physics, at no cost in nominal rotations. A confirmatory, pre-registered test would need more seeds (e.g. 5 L1 and 5 L2 runs, ≈ 10 GPU hours of training).

## E5 — Does the Eureka reward learn faster than the human reward? **Primary criterion NOT met**
Same 10 runs as the matrix (A human / B Eureka, 5 seeds), checkpoints at 100–800 iterations, nominal physics. Mean error (mm) vs iteration — A: 39.8, 43.4, 37.6, 42.7, 41.3, 37.8; B: 57.2, 38.7, 51.6, 42.4, 34.9, 37.7 (at 100, 200, 300, 400, 600, 800). **Primary (learning-curve area of mean error, hypothesis B < A): A 40.75 ± 5.87 vs B 41.43 ± 4.87 mm, diff +0.68 mm, p = 0.84; direction opposite to the hypothesis.** Secondary: error at 100 iterations A 39.8 vs B 57.2 mm (p = 0.29); at 200 A 43.4 vs B 38.7 (p = 0.56); iterations to reach ≤ 45 mm mean error: A [100,100,100,100,200] vs B [100,100,200,200,200]; tail-error curve area p = 0.90; 2 cm curve area A 29.4 vs B 25.9 (p = 0.28). **No evidence that Eureka reaches a given quality earlier; if anything the human reward is nominally faster in the first 100 iterations (not significant).** Caveat unchanged: the Eureka reward-search cost was not counted, which could only make Eureka's overall efficiency look worse. The reward comparison is therefore a null on both final quality (800 iterations) and learning speed.
